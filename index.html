The technology

Imaging designed to come to the patient

When stroke is suspected, every minute matters, and treatment depends on brain imaging first.

EMVision's emu™ is an investigational, portable brain scanner being developed to help clinicians assess suspected stroke at the point of care. It uses low-power electromagnetic waves and AI-based analysis, and is designed to move like a cart ultrasound, operate without shielding or special infrastructure, and scan at the bedside.

How emu™ works
How it works
  1. 1

    Antennas

    An array of proprietary antennas sends pulses of low-power electromagnetic waves into the head.

  2. 2

    Headset

    The waves pass through tissue without ionizing radiation and scatter according to each tissue's dielectric properties. Sensors in the headset capture these interactions.

  3. 3

    Point-of-care neuroimaging

    Physics and data-science techniques are used to reconstruct an image of the brain, and the system is designed to provide AI-based decision support on the device.

Design characteristics

How the system is designed

500–2,000 MHzUltra-high-frequency radio waves, with no ionizing radiation
<10%Scan power compared with a typical cell phone
No shieldingDesigned without special infrastructure or shielding requirements
One headsetFits all head sizes, with pre-set positioning and push-to-scan

Design characteristics as described by EMVision Medical Devices. The emu™ is an investigational device; it is used only in clinical studies, its safety and effectiveness have not been established, and it is not available for sale or for routine clinical care.

Site activation

From agreement to first scan

A clear, repeatable process that respects your team's time and your hospital's standards.

  1. Site assessment

    We review the clinical space, workflow, power, network, and IT requirements with your team.

  2. Delivery & installation

    Our engineers receive, unpack, position, and set up the system on your premises.

  3. Verification

    Commissioning and performance checks confirm the system is ready before the first study scan.

  4. Team training

    Hands-on sessions prepare operators, clinicians, and research staff to scan confidently.

  5. Study start

    We are on site for the first study scans, answering questions and resolving issues in real time.

  6. Ongoing support

    Maintenance, software updates, and remote or on-site support throughout the study.
