Portable brain imaging for the moment stroke is suspected

EMVision is developing world-first bedside electromagnetic brain scanners, currently under clinical investigation, intended to help clinicians assess suspected stroke wherever the patient is.

Why it matters

In stroke care, time is brain

Effective stroke treatments exist, but they are time-sensitive and require brain imaging first.

Knowing a stroke's type, size, severity, and location quickly is essential to choosing the right treatment. By bringing imaging to the bedside, EMVision's technology aims to support earlier diagnosis and better bedside management of stroke patients.

Clot

Ischemic stroke. Patients may benefit from clot-dissolving drugs and clot retrieval.

Bleed

Hemorrhagic stroke. Must be identified quickly because it calls for a different treatment path.

The devices

Two generations of portable neurodiagnostics

emu™ Brain Scanner

EMVision's first-generation point-of-care brain scanner, being developed to detect clinically significant changes at the bedside. It is the investigational device Vita Tech installs and supports at participating U.S. research sites.

  • Portable, like a cart ultrasound
  • No ionizing radiation
  • No shielding or special rooms
  • Designed for bedside use
  • Designed for rapid scanning
  • One headset for all head sizes

First Responder

A proposed ultra-lightweight second-generation scanner designed to be embedded in road and air ambulances, bringing stroke diagnosis to patients before they reach hospital.

Concept device, subject to prototype development and clinical testing.

How it works

A fusion of physics, engineering, and AI

Different brain tissues, including hemorrhage, ischemia, and healthy tissue, interact with electromagnetic waves differently. emu™ is designed to turn those differences into an image.

  • Non-ionizing, ultra-high-frequency radio waves (500–2,000 MHz)
  • Low scan power, under 10% of a typical cell phone (per EMVision)
  • Dielectric contrast (conductivity and permittivity) reflects and phase-shifts the signal
  • Tissue contrast is being investigated as a basis for stroke type classification
  • Designed to show neurodiagnostic output on an onboard display
  1. 1

    Antennas

    An array of proprietary antennas sends pulses of low-power electromagnetic waves into the head.

  2. 2

    Headset

    The waves pass through tissue without ionizing radiation and scatter according to each tissue's dielectric properties. Sensors in the headset capture these interactions.

  3. 3

    Point-of-care neuroimaging

    Physics and data-science techniques are used to reconstruct an image of the brain, and the system is designed to provide AI-based decision support on the device.

Clinical program

Advancing through clinical validation

Vita Tech supports EMVision's prospective, multi-center, blinded pivotal trial of emu™ at comprehensive stroke centers in the United States.

EMVision is working toward a future FDA submission. Our team works with participating hospitals on the ground, from installation and operator training to day-to-day technical support.
